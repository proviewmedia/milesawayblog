Logitech MX Master 2S: The Mouse That Runs My Workflow
By MelvinJuly 17, 2026Gear · 6 min read

I spend most of my working life with a hand on a mouse — designing, editing photos, wrangling files across two computers — so the mouse I use is not a small decision. After trying plenty of them, the one that's earned a permanent spot on my desk is the Logitech MX Master 2S. It's my main mouse, the one I reach for every single day, and it quietly makes everything I do faster and more comfortable. Here's exactly why it earns that spot.

Heads up: this post contains an affiliate link. If you buy through it I may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you — and I only recommend gear I actually use. See my full disclosure.

Built for All-Day Comfort

The first thing you notice is the shape. The MX Master 2S is sculpted for a right hand to fall onto naturally, with a raised arch that supports your palm and a dedicated thumb rest. When you're on a mouse for eight-plus hours, that ergonomics work isn't a luxury — it's the difference between finishing the day loose or with an aching wrist. Months in, my hand still feels good at the end of a long editing session, and that alone justifies the price of admission.

The Scrolling Is the Secret Weapon

Logitech's scroll wheel is the feature I didn't know I needed. It has a SmartShift wheel that switches between a precise, notch-by-notch ratchet mode and a frictionless free-spin — flick it hard and it spins like a fidget wheel, letting you fly down a thousand-line document or a long web page in one motion, then automatically re-engages the ratchet for precise control. There's also a second thumb wheel for horizontal scrolling, which is a genuine gift when you're working across a wide timeline, spreadsheet, or design canvas.

"A great mouse disappears into the work. You stop thinking about the tool and start thinking faster than you could before."

Flow: One Mouse, Two Computers

This is the feature that made the MX Master 2S non-negotiable for me. Logitech Flow lets a single mouse control more than one computer at the same time. Push your cursor to the edge of one screen and it seamlessly jumps onto the second machine — no button, no fuss — and here's the magic: it works across a Mac and a PC. Even better, you can copy and paste text and images, and drag entire files, between the two computers as if they were one. For anyone running a dual-computer setup — a designer, a developer, anyone straddling two operating systems — this quietly erases one of the most annoying friction points in the whole workday.

Endlessly Customizable

Out of the box it's great; with Logitech's software it becomes yours. Every button is remappable — the thumb buttons, the gesture button under the thumb rest, the wheel click — and you can set app-specific profiles so the same button does one thing in Photoshop and another in your browser. Gestures (hold the thumb button and move the mouse) let you manage windows, switch desktops, or zoom without touching the keyboard. You tune it once and it pays you back every day.

Connectivity & Battery

It connects over Bluetooth or the tiny included Unifying USB receiver, and it pairs with up to three devices at once — a button on the bottom cycles between them, so one mouse can run your laptop, desktop, and a work machine. Battery life is a non-issue: a full charge lasts around 70 days, and a quick top-up gives you hours of use from a one-minute charge. I basically never think about charging it, which is exactly how it should be.

MX Master 2S vs 3S — The Honest Note

Logitech has since released the MX Master 3S, and it's worth knowing the difference. The 3S adds near-silent clicks, an electromagnetic MagSpeed scroll wheel, a higher 8,000 DPI sensor, and USB-C charging. All nice upgrades — but the 2S keeps the same excellent ergonomics and the exact same Flow superpower, usually for less money. If you want the quietest possible clicks and USB-C, spring for the 3S. If you want a proven, do-everything productivity mouse at the best value, the 2S is the one I use and happily recommend.

Is It Worth It?

Completely. A mouse is the tool your hand touches more than almost anything else you own, and upgrading from a basic one to the MX Master 2S is one of those changes you feel immediately and never want to undo. Comfortable, precise, endlessly customizable, and — thanks to Flow — genuinely capable of making a two-computer workflow feel like one. If you work on a computer all day, this is the easiest gear upgrade I can point you to.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the MX Master 2S still worth it?

Yes. Even with newer models out, it's an outstanding productivity mouse — comfortable, precise, and customizable — and often cheaper than the latest version. For most people on a computer all day, it's more mouse than they'll ever need.

What is Logitech Flow?

Flow lets one mouse control multiple computers. Move your cursor off the edge of one screen and it jumps to the other machine — even across Mac and PC — and you can copy, paste, and drag files between them.

Does it work with Mac and PC?

Yes — Windows, macOS, and Linux, over Bluetooth or the Unifying receiver, with up to three paired devices. Flow shines specifically in mixed Mac-and-PC setups.

MX Master 2S or 3S?

The 3S adds silent clicks, a MagSpeed wheel, 8,000 DPI, and USB-C. The 2S keeps the same ergonomics and Flow for less. Get the 3S for the quietest clicks and USB-C; get the 2S for the best value on a proven design.

That's my daily driver. It's not flashy, it just makes the work better every single day — which is exactly what the best gear does.
