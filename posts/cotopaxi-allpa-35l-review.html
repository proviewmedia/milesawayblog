Cotopaxi Allpa 35L Travel Pack Review: The One-Bag Clamshell
By MelvinAugust 17, 2026Reviews · 8 min read

The one-bag travel pack is a crowded category full of near-identical black rectangles. The Cotopaxi Allpa is the one that is not black, not shaped like the others, and organised in a way that actually changes how you live out of it. It is also the bag most likely to get a comment at the airport, which you will either enjoy or find exhausting.

Quick Verdict

The best-organised bag in its class. The full clamshell and the mesh compartment system mean you can find anything without unpacking, which is the entire point of travelling from a backpack. The harness is for transit, not trekking, and the boxy shape divides people. Buy it to live out of, not to hike with.

What Makes It Different

Two things. First, it opens completely flat, like a suitcase, rather than through a hole at the top. Second, the main compartment is divided into zippered mesh sections rather than being one cavern.

Those two decisions together are the whole product. You lay the bag flat on a hostel bed, unzip it, and every category of thing is visible at once behind mesh you can see through. Clean clothes here, dirty there, electronics in the smaller one. No digging, no unpacking to reach the bottom, no repacking every morning.

The Allpa 35L. Product image courtesy of Cotopaxi.

Why It Works

The clamshell is not a gimmick

Top-loading backpacks punish you for wanting the thing at the bottom. A clamshell removes that problem entirely. If you have ever emptied a pack onto a bed to find one charger, you already understand why this matters more than any spec sheet number.

The mesh compartments replace packing cubes

You are effectively getting the organisation of a packing cube system built into the bag, which means you are not paying for cubes and not losing volume to their walls. Being able to see through the mesh is the underrated part. You find things by looking rather than by remembering.

The shell holds its shape

The TPU-coated fabric is genuinely tough and sheds rain well, and the bag stands up on its own rather than slumping. That structure is why the clamshell layout works, because a soft bag would just fold in on itself when you opened it.

The warranty is serious

Cotopaxi covers the bag for its lifetime against defects, and the brand is a certified B Corporation that puts a share of revenue into poverty relief. That is not a performance feature, but it is a reasonable part of a buying decision at this price.

"It is built to be lived out of rather than carried far, and it is much better at that job than at the other one."

Where It Falls Short

  • The harness is for transit. There is a removable hipbelt and there are load lifters, which is better than most rivals, but the backpanel is not ventilated and the suspension is not a trekking system. A sweaty back on a long walk is the price.
  • It is boxy. Structure is what makes the layout work, and the cost is a bag that looks like a small suitcase strapped to your shoulders. It does not squash into an undersized sizer or a stuffed overhead bin.
  • Weather resistant, not waterproof. The shell is excellent, the zips are not sealed. Sustained rain will eventually get in through the zips.
  • The colours are loud. For some people that is the appeal. If you would rather not be memorable, the muted colourways exist, but the bag's whole identity is being bright.
  • Thirty-five litres is a real limit. It is a week of clothes with laundry, not a fortnight, and bulky winter layers eat it fast.

Who Should Buy It

Buy it if you want to travel from a backpack for a week or more, move between places often, and value being able to find things instantly. It is close to ideal for city-to-city travel where you are unpacking and repacking constantly.

Skip it if you need a bag that carries comfortably for hours on foot, if you fly budget carriers with tight sizers, or if your trips are short enough that a 30 litre duffel would do the job with less structure and less money.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Cotopaxi Allpa 35L carry-on compliant?

The 35L is built to carry-on dimensions and fits most major carriers comfortably. As always, the risk is budget airlines with smaller sizers, and because the Allpa is structured it does not compress the way a soft duffel does. If you routinely fly low-cost carriers, measure against your specific airline first.

Is 35 litres enough for one-bag travel?

For most people, yes, for trips of a week or more, provided you do laundry. Thirty-five litres is roughly a week of clothes plus electronics if you pack deliberately and use the compartments. It is not enough if you need bulky cold-weather layers or specialist gear, in which case the 42L or 65L versions exist.

Does the Allpa work as a hiking backpack?

Not really, and it is not meant to. The harness has a removable padded hipbelt and load lifters, which is more than most travel packs offer, but the backpanel is not ventilated and the suspension is built for airports rather than trails. It handles a walk from a station to a hostel very well and a mountain much less well.

Is the Cotopaxi Allpa waterproof?

It is weather resistant rather than waterproof. The TPU-coated shell sheds rain well and the fabric itself will not soak through in a shower, but the zips are not sealed. In sustained heavy rain water will eventually find the zips, so use a cover or a liner for anything that must stay dry.
