Samsonite 2-Piece Luggage Set Review: The Cheapest Way Into Hardside
By MelvinAugust 17, 2026Reviews · 7 min read

Buying luggage is one of those purchases where the price range is absurd. The same nominal object, a box on wheels, runs from about $80 to well over $1,000. This Samsonite two-piece set sits at the sensible end, usually around $150 for a carry-on and a larger checked case together, and it is by a wide margin the most popular luggage we see people buy.

Quick Verdict

The right buy if you fly a few times a year and want matching hardside cases without overthinking it. You are getting an honest product at an honest price. If you fly monthly, spend more now, because the wheels and handle on budget luggage are what give out first.

What You Get

Two hardside spinner cases, a carry-on and a larger checked-size case, both on four-wheel spinner bases, both with telescoping handles and a textured shell that hides scuffs better than a gloss finish would. They nest, so the carry-on stores inside the larger case and the set takes up one case worth of closet space rather than two.

The two-piece set. Product image courtesy of Samsonite.

Why It Works

Two cases for the price of one mid-range case

This is the actual argument. A single mid-tier hardside carry-on frequently costs more than this entire set. If you currently own no decent luggage, buying a matched pair in one go solves the problem completely rather than halfway.

Four-wheel spinners are worth having

Spinners roll upright beside you instead of dragging behind at an angle, which matters more than it sounds like it should in a crowded airport or a narrow train aisle. Two-wheel cases are more durable, but they put the weight on your wrist for the whole walk.

Hardside earns its keep on checked bags

Anything going in a cargo hold gets thrown. A hard shell distributes that better than fabric, seals against rain on the tarmac, and wipes clean afterwards. For the checked case in particular, hardside is the right call.

The brand is not decoration

Samsonite has been making luggage for over a century, and the practical benefit is that spare parts and warranty service actually exist. With no-name luggage a broken wheel means a new suitcase.

"Wheels and handles fail long before shells do. That is where the money goes in expensive luggage, and it is where it was saved in cheap luggage."

Where It Falls Short

  • The wheels are the weak point. They are fine, not excellent. On a set at this price the wheels and the telescoping handle are the first things to fail, and they will fail before the shell shows any real damage.
  • Hardside means no give. There is no squeezing an extra jacket in and no external pocket for the thing you need at security. If you habitually overpack, a soft case is more forgiving.
  • The shell scratches. The texture disguises it well, and it is cosmetic rather than structural, but do not expect it to look new after three trips in a hold.
  • Check the carry-on against your airline. It meets standard US carry-on dimensions, but several budget European and Asian carriers use smaller sizers, and a hard case cannot be persuaded.

Who Should Buy It

Buy it if you travel a few times a year, currently own nothing decent, and want the whole problem solved in one purchase. It is also a genuinely good first set for someone just starting to travel properly.

Skip it if you fly most months, in which case the extra spend on a better wheel and handle assembly pays for itself. Skip it too if you are a committed carry-on-only traveller, because then you want one excellent cabin bag rather than two average cases, and you would be better served by something like a convertible duffel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Samsonite 2-piece set good quality for the price?

For around $150 for two cases, yes, with the usual caveats of the price bracket. You get a genuine hardside shell, four-wheel spinners on both cases and Samsonite backing. What you do not get is the shell thickness, the wheel quality or the warranty length of their premium lines. It is good value, not a bargain version of an expensive suitcase.

Is the carry-on in the set actually carry-on size?

It is built to standard domestic US carry-on dimensions and fits the major US carriers. Be careful with budget European and Asian carriers, several of which use smaller sizers, and remember that hardside cases cannot be squeezed the way a soft bag can. If it does not fit the sizer, it does not fit.

Hardside or softside luggage, which is better?

Hardside protects fragile things better, wipes clean and generally seals better against rain. Softside gives you external pockets, absorbs an overpacking mistake and squeezes into tight overhead bins. For most short-haul travel softside is more forgiving. For anything fragile or anything checked, hardside wins.

How long will a budget hardside suitcase last?

If you travel a few times a year and treat it normally, several years is realistic. The failure points are almost always the wheels and the telescoping handle rather than the shell. If you fly monthly, expect to replace a set at this price within a couple of years and consider spending more up front.
