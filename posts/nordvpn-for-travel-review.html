Is a VPN Worth It for Travel? An Honest NordVPN Review
By MelvinAugust 17, 2026Reviews · 7 min read

The travel VPN pitch usually leans on fear, with a story about a hacker in a cafe stealing your bank login over the wifi. That story is mostly out of date, and it is worth saying so before recommending anything. The honest case for a VPN while travelling is quieter and more practical than the marketing, but it does exist.

Quick Verdict

Worth it if you work from public wifi regularly, want your browsing private from hotel and airport networks, or want a shot at your home streaming abroad. Not worth it if you mostly use your own mobile data. Buy the two-year plan or do not bother, because the monthly price is poor value.

What a VPN Actually Does

It builds an encrypted tunnel between your device and a server somewhere else, then sends your traffic out from there. Two consequences follow. The network you are physically on cannot see what you are doing, and the sites you visit see the server's location rather than yours.

That is the whole product. Everything else is a consequence of those two facts.

The Honest Security Case

Here is the part most reviews skip. Nearly every site and app you use already encrypts its traffic with HTTPS. Someone sharing a cafe network genuinely cannot read your banking password out of the air the way they might have in 2012. That threat has largely been engineered away.

What remains real is smaller but not nothing:

  • Evil twin hotspots. Anyone can name a hotspot "Hotel Guest Wifi" and wait. Connect to the wrong one and your traffic runs through a stranger's equipment. A VPN makes that traffic useless to them.
  • The network operator can see where you go. HTTPS hides the contents of a page, not which sites you visited. Whoever runs the hotel network can build that list.
  • Some networks interfere. Hotel and airport networks sometimes inject content, throttle services or block things arbitrarily. A tunnel routes around all of it.

The everyday risk is not a hacker in the corner. It is not knowing who runs the network.

Why NordVPN Specifically

It is fast enough to forget about

The failure mode of a bad VPN is that you turn it off because everything crawls, at which point you are paying for nothing. NordVPN's protocol is quick enough that most people leave it on permanently, which is the only way it protects anything.

The server network is genuinely large

Thousands of servers across sixty-odd countries means there is usually one near wherever you are, and being near matters more for speed than any other single factor.

It handles streaming better than most

Streaming services fight VPNs continuously. Nord loses that fight less often than the competition. Treat it as a bonus rather than a guarantee, because any given server can stop working without warning.

Kill switch and multiple devices

The kill switch cuts your connection entirely if the tunnel drops, so you never silently fall back to the open network. One subscription covers enough devices to include a partner's phone and a laptop.

Where It Falls Short

  • The monthly price is bad value. Like the whole industry, the economics only work on a long plan. If you will not commit to two years, the maths is unconvincing.
  • Renewal pricing jumps. The headline discount is an introductory rate. Set a calendar reminder before renewal rather than being surprised.
  • Streaming access is not promised. Do not buy it primarily for this.
  • It is not anonymity. A VPN hides you from the network, not from an account you are logged into. Signing into your own accounts identifies you regardless.
  • Some countries restrict VPNs. Check the local rules before you travel.

Who Should Skip It

If you travel with a travel eSIM and mostly use your own mobile data, you are already on a private connection and a VPN adds little. Buy the eSIM first. It solves a bigger problem for less money, and it is the better purchase if you only make one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need a VPN when travelling?

It depends on what you do. If you only use your own mobile data and apps like WhatsApp and banking, which are already encrypted, the security benefit is modest. If you regularly work from hotel, cafe and airport wifi, or you want to reach services from home that are geo-restricted abroad, a VPN earns its place. It is useful rather than essential for most travellers.

Is public wifi actually dangerous?

Less than it used to be. Nearly all websites and apps now use HTTPS, which already encrypts your traffic, so the classic story of someone reading your passwords in a cafe is largely outdated. The remaining real risks are fake hotspots impersonating a hotel network and having your browsing visible to whoever runs the network. A VPN addresses both.

Can I use a VPN to watch my home streaming abroad?

Sometimes, and it is not guaranteed. Streaming services actively detect and block VPN traffic, so a server that works this month may not next month. NordVPN performs better than most here, but treat access as a bonus and not a reason to buy.

Is a VPN legal?

In most countries yes, and it is a normal business tool. A handful of countries including China, Russia, Iran and the UAE restrict or ban VPN use to varying degrees. Check the rules for your destination before you travel rather than assuming.
