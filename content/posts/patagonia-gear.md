<h2>Why Patagonia (Beyond the Logo)</h2>
<p>Let's get the honest part out of the way: Patagonia is expensive. But it's one of the few brands where I think the premium is genuinely justified, and it comes down to three things. First, the gear <strong>lasts</strong> — I have pieces that look nearly new after years of hard use. Second, when something does wear out, Patagonia <strong>repairs it</strong>, often for free, through their Worn Wear program and Ironclad Guarantee; they would rather fix your jacket than sell you a new one. Third, the company actually walks the environmental talk — recycled and Fair Trade materials, and 1% of sales to the planet.</p>
<p>Add it up and the math flips: on a <em>cost-per-wear</em> basis, my Patagonia has been some of the cheapest clothing I own, because I wear it for years instead of replacing it every season. That's the lens I'd use on everything below.</p>

<blockquote><p>"Buy it once, wear it for a decade, and get it repaired when it finally shows its age. That's the whole pitch — and Patagonia actually delivers it."</p></blockquote>
