<h2>How an eSIM Actually Works</h2>

<p>Your phone has a physical SIM slot and, if it is reasonably modern, an embedded SIM that can be programmed by software. You buy a plan online, receive a QR code, scan it, and your phone now has a second line. Your home number stays active on the original SIM for calls and texts, while data routes over the new one.</p>

<p>The practical benefit is that you set it up on your own wifi at home, days before you fly, and simply switch it on when you land. No queue, no passport photocopy, no language barrier at a kiosk, and no tiny plastic tray tool to lose.</p>

<h2>The Things Nobody Warns You About</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Data-only means no phone number.</strong> Neither gives you a local number on standard plans. Fine for WhatsApp and maps, not fine if a bank wants to SMS you a code. Keep your home line active for that.</li>
  <li><strong>Install before you fly.</strong> You generally need internet to install the eSIM. Doing it at home on wifi is easy. Doing it in an arrivals hall with no data is a problem you created for yourself.</li>
  <li><strong>Turn off data roaming on your home line.</strong> This is the mistake that costs real money. People install an eSIM, feel clever, and then their primary SIM quietly roams in the background all week.</li>
  <li><strong>Check your phone is unlocked.</strong> A carrier-locked phone may refuse the eSIM, and these are usually non-refundable once installed.</li>
  <li><strong>Buy small, top up later.</strong> Estimating your data needs upward is how you overpay. Topping up takes seconds.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What is a travel eSIM and how does it work?</h3>
<p>An eSIM is a SIM card built into your phone that can be programmed by software instead of swapped physically. You buy a data plan online, scan a QR code, and your phone gains a second line for that country or region. There is nothing to collect, nothing to post, and nothing to lose, which is the entire appeal over a physical local SIM.</p>

<h3>Do travel eSIMs give you a phone number?</h3>
<p>Usually not. Both Airalo and AirHub sell data-only plans, which means no local number for calls or SMS. In practice this matters less than it sounds, because messaging and calls run over WhatsApp, iMessage and similar. It does matter if you need to receive an SMS verification code, so keep your home line active for that.</p>

<h3>Is an eSIM cheaper than roaming?</h3>
<p>Almost always, and often dramatically. Carrier roaming day-passes commonly run about $10 to $12 per day, so a two week trip can pass $150. A regional eSIM with a sensible data allowance for the same period is frequently under $40. The exception is if your home plan already includes free international data, in which case use it.</p>

<h3>Does my phone support eSIM?</h3>
<p>Most phones released from roughly 2019 onward do, including iPhone XS and newer, recent Google Pixel, and most flagship Samsung Galaxy models. Your phone also has to be carrier unlocked. Check both before buying anything, because an eSIM is non-refundable once installed.</p>
